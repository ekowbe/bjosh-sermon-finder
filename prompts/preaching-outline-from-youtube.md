# Prompt: YouTube sermon → preaching outline

Paste this into any assistant that can access YouTube (claude.ai with web access, ChatGPT, a
transcript tool + LLM, etc.). Swap the URL on the first line to reuse it for another sermon.

---

Get the full transcript of this sermon: https://youtu.be/QZ99-2aLMEQ

Prefer the official captions; if only auto-generated captions exist, use those and clean them up
(add punctuation, fix obvious mis-transcriptions, and convert spoken scripture references like
"first Corinthians chapter two verse nine" into standard form like 1 Corinthians 2:9).

Then act as an experienced homiletics assistant and turn the transcript into a **preaching
outline note** — something I can preach from at a pulpit, not a summary I'd read at a desk.

**Faithfulness rules (most important):**
- Outline only what the preacher actually said. Do not invent points, add scriptures he never
  cited, or "improve" his argument.
- Quote memorable lines **verbatim** in quotation marks.
- If a scripture reference or name is garbled in the captions and you're not certain, keep it
  but mark it with (?).

**Format the note exactly like this:**

1. **Title** — the sermon's own title if stated, otherwise a short title drawn from its
   central message.
2. **Main text(s)** — the anchor scripture(s) the sermon is built on.
3. **Big idea** — the whole sermon in one sentence.
4. **Outline** — the sermon's main points, numbered, in the order preached. For each point:
   - a one-line statement of the point (punchy, preachable wording)
   - the scriptures cited under it
   - any story, illustration, or example he used (one line each, enough to retell it)
   - 1–2 verbatim quotes worth repeating
   - the approximate timestamp where the point begins
5. **Application / charge** — the specific things the congregation was told to do or become.
6. **Closing** — how he landed the sermon (prayer, altar call, final exhortation).
7. **One-line takeaway** — what a listener should still remember a week later.

**Style:** short lines, bold the scripture references, no paragraphs longer than two
sentences — it must be skimmable mid-preaching. If the video turns out not to be a sermon,
say so and stop instead of forcing the format.
